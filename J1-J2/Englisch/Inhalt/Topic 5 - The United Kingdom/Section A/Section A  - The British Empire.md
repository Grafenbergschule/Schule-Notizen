Outline the suffering and effects of slavery during colonial times, after abolition and in the present day. 

#### **1. During colonial times**

- **Appalling transportation conditions:** Slaves were shackled and transported under horrific, disease-ridden conditions aboard ships.

- **Physical and emotional cruelty:** Those who survived the journey endured unspeakable physical and emotional abuse on the plantations.

- **High mortality rate:** Countless lives were lost during transport and forced labor.

- **Inhumane working conditions:** Enslaved people were forced to work under severe conditions on sugar plantations across the Caribbean (e.g., Jamaica, Barbados).


#### **2. After abolition**

- **Abject poverty and lack of education:** Former slaves were left in extreme poverty; for example, in Jamaica, 80% of the population lacked functional literacy when the country gained independence in 1962.

- **Continued forced labor:** After slavery was officially abolished, former slaves were compelled to work for another four years without pay to further "compensate" their owners.

- **Financial compensation for slave owners:** British slave owners received huge financial compensations from the government (amounting to 40% of government expenditure in 1834), while the victims received nothing.


#### **3. In the present day**

- **Economic disparities:** Wealth extracted from African slaves continues to contribute to British society's prosperity, while former colonies still suffer from these historical scars.

- **Visible legacy in infrastructure and institutions:** The wealth generated by the slave trade remains visible today in the architecture of British cities (such as London, Liverpool, and Bristol) and in long-established companies and institutions.

- **Global inequality:** The structural inequalities that divide the Global North and Global South stem directly from the deep injustices established during the era of slavery and colonialism.

In the opening section, the author contrasts the national awareness of slavery in the United States with that of Great Britain. While the U.S. has openly acknowledged the profound impact of slavery on its history and infrastructure, British politicians have systematically ignored the country's role as a major perpetrator. However, recent research demonstrates that British slave owners generated immense wealth by forcibly transporting millions of African slaves to Caribbean plantations, leaving behind a legacy that directly shapes modern Britain.

Following abolition, the situation did not improve immediately for former slaves. Furthermore, the British government paid vast sums of financial compensation to slave owners, while leaving former slaves in abject poverty and without access to basic education. As a result, the extracted wealth built iconic British architecture, enriched major financial institutions, and fueled modern economic prosperity, while leaving former colonies economically disadvantaged.

In conclusion, the author emphasizes that Britain must incorporate its true colonial history into its national consciousness. Rather than viewing history through a sanitized lens, the country needs to recognize how past injustices continue to drive present global inequalities between the Global North and South, enabling Britain to understand its ethical responsibilities in the modern world.
